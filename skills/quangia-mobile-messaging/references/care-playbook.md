# Care Playbook

Read this reference when writing a seven-day trial message, plan-purchase follow-up, activation message, or renewal care message.

## Evidence Before Copy

Use only facts available in the current tenant:

- `USER.md`: preferred address, family members, stated priorities, important constraints.
- Recent conversation or session summary: questions asked, work completed, unresolved concerns, tone and engagement.
- Tool results: reminders actually created, files actually delivered, schedules actually found.
- Subscription record: exact plan, amount, payment state, activation state, and expiration date.

Choose the single fact most useful to the customer now. Do not force personalization with a name alone.

## Seven-Day Journey

### Day 1 - Remove One Mental Burden

Emotional truth: new customers are curious but do not want another app to learn.

- If a real obligation is known, offer to turn it into one reminder or recurring routine.
- Otherwise ask for the one thing they are most afraid of forgetting this week.
- CTA example: `Gia chủ chỉ cần nhắn một việc + thời gian, Quản Gia sẽ sắp lịch ngay.`

### Day 2 - Help A Child Move Forward

Emotional truth: parents want children to understand, not become dependent on answers.

- Refer to a verified child, grade, subject, or recent exercise when available.
- Explain the Socratic benefit in one sentence, then invite one photo or one question.
- Never claim a child is struggling unless the family said so.

### Day 3 - Reduce Repeated Friction

Emotional truth: repeated reminders exhaust both parents and children.

- Use a known routine such as study time, chores, bedtime, or screen time.
- Offer a tiny routine with two or three steps, not a full household system.
- CTA should let the parent provide one routine and one preferred time.

### Day 4 - Make One Adult Task Lighter

Emotional truth: family administration is often invisible but mentally heavy.

- Choose one relevant expense, document, purchase list, appointment, or work task.
- Offer a concrete output: a short budget, checklist, summary, or scheduled reminder.
- Avoid introducing finance if the customer has shown no need; use the most relevant adult task instead.

### Day 5 - Make Progress Visible

Emotional truth: customers stay when they can see what became easier.

- Reflect one verified result from the first four days.
- Name the human benefit, not the feature: fewer reminders to remember, a child thinking independently, a clearer plan.
- If there was no activity, say so gently and offer the easiest quick win. Never fabricate a result.

### Day 6 - Create A Shared Moment

Emotional truth: useful technology should leave more attention for the family.

- Build one realistic weekend or connection idea from verified time, budget, location, age, and preferences.
- If those facts are missing, ask for no more than two inputs in one short line.

### Day 7 - Close With Evidence, Not Pressure

Emotional truth: the customer is deciding whether Quangia deserves a place in family life.

- Summarize no more than three verified outcomes or needs discovered.
- Recommend the smallest suitable continuation option and connect it to the most important ongoing need.
- State terms only from subscription data. Do not create urgency, scarcity, or fear.
- Give two easy choices when appropriate: continue with help choosing a plan, or receive a concise weekly summary first.

## Purchase And Renewal States

### Considering

Reflect the need the customer is trying to solve. Recommend the smallest suitable plan, explain one relevant benefit, and invite one decision question.

### Payment Pending

Put the verified plan and amount near the top. Give one numbered next step. Ask about the actual obstacle rather than repeating a generic payment reminder.

### Activated

Confirm the verified plan and validity briefly. Translate the purchase into one first outcome the customer can use today. Do not make the whole message a receipt.

### Renewal Approaching

Use the verified expiration date and one capability or routine the family actually uses. Explain continuity calmly and provide one clear renewal action.

### Declined Or Expired

Do not guilt, threaten data loss, or create fear. Preserve dignity, offer a useful summary or next step, and leave the door open without repeated pressure.

## Copy Pattern

Use this flexible rhythm, not a rigid template:

1. One-line hook tied to the customer's situation.
2. One or two short lines showing understanding or verified progress.
3. A short label or up to three bullets for the useful action.
4. One low-effort CTA specific to the situation.

Vary the opening, label, and CTA across days. A new day must not look like the previous message with only the feature name changed.
